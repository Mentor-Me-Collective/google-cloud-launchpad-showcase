# Problem evidence and SDG 13 framing

## Problem statement (hypothesis)

During extreme heat, a time-pressured manager of multiple facilities may struggle to synthesize current weather conditions, determine which readiness gaps matter first and convert recommendations into assigned, auditable work. ClimateOps targets this *coordination friction*, not weather prediction or clinical health treatment. Interviews and measurements are **not yet available**; do not present this problem as experimentally confirmed.

## Sources and correct interpretation

1. **NWS operational weather:** https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api and https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-alerts. Use live alert identifiers, timing and official wording; source records determine factual weather claims.
2. **NYC Heat Vulnerability Index:** https://a816-dohbesp.nyc.gov/IndicatorPublic/data-features/hvi/ . Neighborhood index context is not an observed facility readiness score. Identify precise neighborhood mapping and publication date.
3. **NYC cooling center information:** https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02663 . A historical public listing does **not** certify current cooling-center status or hours; verify present information and never imply partnership.
4. **Source facility directories:** select and record URL, date, organization, coordinates and verification method; no real facilities have yet been sourced in this package.
5. **Interviews/workflow observation:** prospective evidence only; no participants or outcomes claimed.

## Research questions

- What are the first three facts a manager needs from a weather event?
- How do staff shortages affect work selection, assignment and proof?
- How are approvals made and recorded now?
- What level of citation detail makes a readiness recommendation credible?
- How can the workflow work without personal identifiers or sensitive photos?

## Assumptions to test

A1: manager can review within two minutes for a scripted scenario. A2: the facility list and task status are sufficient for prioritization. A3: a verified checklist/photo improves closure quality over an unchecked task. A4: model-generated text saves effort without compromising citation correctness. Each requires actual evaluation before being described as a finding.

## Intended outcomes and limits

Measure preparation time, completion of approval-to-proof journey, citation accuracy and user comprehension. Do **not** claim avoided injury/death, saved lives, actual readiness gains or impact on neighborhood vulnerability from a prototype demonstration.
